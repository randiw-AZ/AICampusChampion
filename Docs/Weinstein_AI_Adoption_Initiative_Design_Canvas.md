**AI Adoption Initiative Design Canvas**  
   
Teaching Resources and Guidance  
Department of Physiology, College of Medicine  
Randi B. Weinstein, Ph.D.  
   
   
**Problem Statement**  
   
The goal of this AI Adoption Initiative is to help Department of Physiology faculty create cohesive, intentional, and meaningful guidance for our teaching mission. While the university provides resources and syllabus guidance ([https://responsibleai.arizona.edu/faculty-staff/teaching-learning](https://responsibleai.arizona.edu/faculty-staff/teaching-learning)), faculty are still struggling independently with how to respond to and incorporate generative AI.  
   
**Target Audience**  
   
All faculty in the Department of Physiology who are teaching undergraduate or graduate students  
   
*Mixed AI fluency;* some use generative AI extensively, some are hesitant about AI usage and implementation, and most are in the middle  
   
*What they need:* Cohesive guidance about how to respond to and how to use generative AI in their teaching. 

They also need to put generative AI in education and the workforce into a larger national and global context.

*Key concerns they have:* academic integrity, privacy, FERPA compliance, environmental concerns, disclosure, preparation for workforce  
   
   
**AI Tools and Approach**  
   
Consideration of AI tools will start with the University of Arizona AI Tools & Data Use Guide ([https://responsibleai.arizona.edu/ai-tools-guide](https://responsibleai.arizona.edu/ai-tools-guide)). Faculty will explore Tools provided by the University of Arizona Enterprise agreements with Google (Gemini and Google Notebook), Microsoft (CoPilot), and Amazon Web Services (U of A GenAI platform). Restricted tools (Turnitin AI detection feature; [https://deanofstudents.arizona.edu/artificial-intelligence-ai](https://deanofstudents.arizona.edu/artificial-intelligence-ai)) will be discussed. Additional tools can be examined as requested by faculty. 

   
**Ethical Safeguards**  
   
Faculty will explore the NIST Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework and UNESCO Recommendations on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence.  
Risks: Based on the NIST AI RMF, the three most relevant trustworthiness properties for AI systems in education are: 1\) Privacy-Enhanced, 2\) Fair \- with Harmful Bias Managed, and 3\) Secure and Resilient. Privacy-Enhanced is important to protect anonymity and confidentiality of information. Fair is important to prevent bias and discrimination. Secure and resilient is important to maintain functionality and protect against unauthorized access or attacks. 

UNESCO addresses Human rights and dignity as foundations, the environment as a core value, policy recommendations for gender, culture, education, and health, global solidarity and equity, and psychological and relational impacts.

*Responsible party:* As a group, we will identify and evaluate the biggest risks associated with generative AI to determine whether the risks outweigh the benefits. Any questions or unresolved issues will be referred to the University of Arizona Office of Responsible AI ([https://responsibleai.arizona.edu/ORAI](https://responsibleai.arizona.edu/ORAI)). 

   
**Equity considerations**  
   
*Population 1:* Faculty who are not interested in incorporating AI into their teaching. Adjustment: Participation is voluntary. We will create a safe environment where those with less interest can join the conversation if they choose.  
   
*Population 2:* Faculty who are overwhelmed by the challenges of generative AI and don’t know how to get started. Adjustment: Topics should be covered at a range of levels to include foundational knowledge and provide opportunity for more advanced exploration.  
   
   
**Success metrics**  
   
*Metric:* 3 meetings during the Fall 2026 semester with 4-5 Department of Physiology teaching faculty  
   
*How measured:* Meeting dates and attendance; self-reported deployment of guidance or tools for teaching faculty  
   
*Timeline:* 3 meetings between August and December, 2025  
   
*Minimum acceptable threshold*: Creation of an open dialog about the challenges posed by generative AI and strategies or guidance to follow  
   
   
**Rollout Plan – First Three Steps**  
   
*Step 1:* What, Who, Resource needed, By when: 

Request time from the Department Head for time at the August Faculty Retreat to present an overview of the AI Champions program and present my AI Adoption Initiative. This has already been completed.

*Step 2*: What, Who, Resource needed, By when:

Schedule at least 2 meetings during the Fall 2026 semester with teaching faculty to review our AI guidance needs, to explore the University of Arizona resources and AI Tools & Data Use Guide, and to draft an AI guidance policy for our courses. Scheduling should be within the first month of the semester.

*Step 3:* What, Who, Resource needed, By when: 

Coordinate the scheduled meetings and provide written summaries of the outcomes by the end of the Fall 2026 semester.
